# AMooreCreativeCodeProject2
Project 2 for Creative Code, an L5 assignment built around the theme of patterns and randomness.

Artist Statement

This work is meant to illustrate the idea of order and life overcoming chaos and lifelessness. The darker, "chaos" side of the work has a subtle movement inspired by water, while the green network on the “order” side is inspired by the structures of atoms and molecules. Naturally, I made the chaos side’s movements completely random. Meanwhile the order side has a degree of randomization to simulate the complexity of life, but with restrains on where the circles generate to maintain a degree of consistency each time the program is run.

The Longer, More Philosophical Artist Statement

"In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth. The earth was without form and void, and darkness was over the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God was hovering over the face of the waters." The themes of chaos and order are very fundamental to the Bible's account of the beginning of the world. The initial chaotic state of the world is not bad, but God does not pronounce it to be "good" either. Instead, he goes about bringing order to the world through boundaries, allotments, meaning, and purpose. And with order comes Life. Life is complex beyond our capacity to fully understand, but it is certainly not chaotic. Our bodies run due to millions of minuscule mechanisms, built upon the architecture of atoms and molecules. There is even order in the metaphysical aspects of the world, allowing us to attempt to understand logic, philosophy, and wisdom. This is the reality that God constructs in Genesis, built according to his will and Word. But this reality is not empty. God creates images of himself, lesser beings with their own wills and agency, desiring that they work alongside him in continuing to bring order to the universe. But instead, the cataclysmic occurs: these image bearers, humans, choose to disobey their creator, directing their wills against the order he has made, turning it back into chaos. And by breaking the metaphysical order of reality, humans cause the physical order to beginning breaking down as well, causing our universe to tend towards chaos. Chaos in and of itself is not wrong, but the deliberate degeneration of order back into chaos is; today we call this movement Death. Our whole universe is in a state of slow death. Structures crumble. Stars burn out. Life decays.
These themes of chaos and order, death and life become the basis of the entire Bible. The rest of the Bible after Genesis could be accurately described as the chronicles of a post-apocalyptic death world. It is a broken world built by humans with broken natures. A world tainted by the corruption of death. Not merely physical death, but spiritual death as well: manifesting as selfishness, hatred, deceit, cruelty and a million other symptoms. It is a world which prophecy has declared will be purged by fire, so that a new world of order and life can be made—a world without chaos, without death, without evil or corruption or hate or pain. But there is a problem: the chronicle recounts that the corruption of death has become bound up in the nature of humans, who simply cannot seem to completely rid themselves of it. It seems that humanity, too, will be purged by fire. But God then presents a strange offer: if humans will give their nature of death to him, the nature that they forged by their own wills all the way back in Genesis, he will give them his own nature of order and life. Thus, it is not humanity who burns as chaos and death are purged: it is God himself, in the person of the Son. In fact, the Bible declares that this has already been done: the offer is in place, and open. At this point, it is merely a matter of choice, as it was in the beginning: the choice to align oneself with the God of order, and move from death to life; or to continue to move against order, evermore on in the decaying nature of death.
